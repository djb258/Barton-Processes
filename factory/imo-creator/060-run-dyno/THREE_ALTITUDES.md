# Three Altitudes

## The structural axes of any system

one for each of the Three Primitives (Thing / Flow / Change). These are not the
4-tier cadence-based UI altitudes (50K / 30K / 10K / 5K, see
`ALTITUDE_LEGEND.md`); these are the **structural axes** derived from

## §1 The Three Altitudes (table)

| Altitude | Primitive | Question Answered |
|---|---|---|
| **Stratification Layer** | Thing | What exists at this depth? |
| **Information Flow** | Flow | What moves between depths? |
| **State Transition** | Change | What flips at this depth? |

The comparator that locks each altitude is its primitive. Anything reducing to
**bounded existence** is Stratification Layer (Thing). Anything reducing to
**directed passage** is Information Flow (Flow). Anything reducing to
**discrete alteration** is State Transition (Change). Three primitives. Three
altitudes. No fourth.

## §2 Stratification Layer (Thing)

Stratification Layer is the locked constant that names the fact of vertically ordered containment within any system—the structural truth that things nest inside other things at distinct altitudes, and that these altitudes are not arbitrary but load-bearing. A Stratification Layer is a discrete horizontal plane of organization where entities of comparable scope, grain, or authority reside together, bounded above by a layer of greater abstraction and below by a layer of greater specificity. It is not a gradient; it is a floor. Each layer is a thing in its own right—identifiable, bounded, persistent—while simultaneously serving as the housing for the things that populate it.

From this altitude, a reader observes separation made visible. One sees that a system is not a flat field of undifferentiated parts but a stack of distinct shelves, each shelf holding components that share a common resolution. In insurance, one layer holds individual policy clauses, another holds product structures, another holds regulatory regimes. In software, one layer holds machine instructions, another holds application logic, another holds user-facing interfaces. In biology, one layer holds molecules, another holds organelles, another holds tissues. The observer at any given layer sees peers laterally and recognizes that looking up or down requires a shift in grain—a crossing of a boundary that is itself real and structural.

The role Stratification Layer plays in the larger architecture is enforcement of coherence under complexity. Without it, a system collapses into a single undifferentiated mass where everything references everything at every scale. Stratification prevents this by imposing jurisdictional boundaries: what belongs at one altitude does not float freely to another. It enables delegation downward and abstraction upward. It is the reason a system can be described, navigated, and governed at multiple resolutions without contradiction.

The derivation chain runs directly and exclusively to Thing. A Stratification Layer is not a movement between positions—that would make it Flow. It is not a transition from one state to another—that would make it Change. It is a bounded, identifiable, persistent structural entity: a container with edges, existing at a fixed altitude, holding other things within it. It is a thing that organizes things, and its thingness is what makes the vertical order of a system real rather than merely metaphorical.

## §3 Information Flow (Flow)

Information Flow is the structural constant that captures the movement of distinguishable states between bounded regions within any system. It is not the content of what moves, nor the transformation that content undergoes upon arrival, but the transit itself — the directed passage of pattern from one locus to another across a gradient of access. Where there is a sender, a receiver, and a channel that connects them such that the receiver's state space becomes constrained by the sender's state space, Information Flow is present. It is pure conveyance of distinction.

From this altitude, what is observable is directionality, bandwidth, latency, and fidelity. A reader sees that something knowable at one location becomes knowable at another location, and can measure how much, how fast, how accurately, and through what path. One observes channels opening and closing, congestion and clarity, signal reaching its destination intact or degraded. In an insurance system, this appears as a claim report traveling from policyholder to adjuster. In biology, it is a hormone reaching a receptor cell. In software, it is a packet traversing a network stack. The reader does not yet see what the receiver does with what arrives — that belongs to Change. The reader sees only that the passage occurred and can characterize its properties.

The role Information Flow plays in the larger architecture is connective. It is what makes distributed systems coherent rather than merely colocated. Without it, Things exist in isolation and Change remains local. Information Flow is the mechanism by which one region of a system becomes coupled to another, enabling coordination, feedback, dependency, and propagation. It is the substrate upon which control, signaling, and communication are built at higher tiers.

The derivation chain to its primitive is direct and singular. Flow is the primitive of directed passage — anything that moves from here to there along a path reduces to Flow. Information Flow specifies what is flowing: distinguishable states, pattern, constraint on possibility. It does not reduce to Thing because it is not a bounded entity at rest; information in transit is not an object but a movement. It does not reduce to Change because nothing is being transformed in the act of transit itself — the pattern is preserved, not altered. The essence is passage, and passage is Flow.

## §4 State Transition (Change)

State Transition is the locked constant that captures the discrete moment when a system element passes from one defined condition to another, with no intermediate ambiguity. It is not the condition before, not the condition after, and not the passage of time or material between them — it is the structural fact that a boundary has been crossed irreversibly within the frame of observation. At this altitude, State Transition names the smallest recognizable unit of Change that a system can register: the point at which what was true is no longer true and what is now true has replaced it.

What is observable from this altitude is a before-configuration and an after-configuration with a clean seam between them. A policy moves from active to lapsed. A parcel moves from unencumbered to encumbered. A cell moves from undifferentiated to committed. A software object moves from initialized to running. The observer does not need to see the internal mechanics of how the crossing occurred; what is visible is that the system's ledger now records a different entry than it did one moment prior. The transition itself is the observable — not gradual drift, not tendency, but the registrable flip.

The role State Transition plays in the larger architecture is that of the atomic event marker. Without it, no system can distinguish history from present, no process can checkpoint, and no rule can fire conditionally. It is the mechanism by which Change becomes countable, sequenceable, and governable. Every workflow, every contract clause triggered by condition, every biological signaling cascade depends on the system's ability to recognize that a state transition has occurred and to treat it as a fact upon which subsequent logic can operate.

The derivation chain runs exclusively to Change because State Transition does not require a second entity (which would invoke Thing) nor does it require a medium or conduit (which would invoke Flow). It requires only that something that was one way is now another way — pure alteration of condition. Flow may carry the cause, and Thing may be the subject, but the transition itself is nothing other than the primitive Change made concrete and singular: one discrete, irreducible shift, isolated and named.

## §5 The 4×3 Matrix — Cadence × Structural

The Three Altitudes (this doc) and the Four Cadence Tiers (`ALTITUDE_LEGEND.md`)
are **orthogonal axes**. Every artifact carries both tags. Together they form
a **4×3 matrix** — 12 cells total. All three structural altitudes exist at
every cadence level; what shifts is **dominance** — which structural altitude
is most common and most observable at that cadence.

### §5.1 The Matrix

| Cadence | Years (50K) | Quarters (30K) | Months (10K) | Cycles/Events (5K) |
|---------|-------------|----------------|--------------|--------------------|
| **Stratification Layer** (Thing) | **DOMINANT** | **DOMINANT** | present | present |
| **Information Flow** (Flow) | rare | present | **DOMINANT** | present |
| **State Transition** (Change) | rare | present | present | **DOMINANT** |

### §5.2 Dominance Pattern

| Cadence | Dominant Structural Altitude | Why |
|---------|------------------------------|-----|
| **50K** strategic / years | **Stratification Layer** (Thing) | At yearly cadence, almost everything is a bounded entity that persists. Trunks, hubs, structural categories. Things dominate. Flows and Changes exist (a multi-year information pipeline; a once-a-decade strategic shift) but they are rare. |
| **30K** tactical / quarters | **Stratification Layer** (Thing) | Operator hubs, branch structures, quarterly business units. Still mostly Things. |
| **10K** operational / months | **Information Flow** (Flow) | Process mechanics. ETL pipelines, monthly data movements, integration paths. Flows dominate because operations *are* flows at monthly cadence. |
| **5K** execution / cycles | **State Transition** (Change) | Events firing, states flipping, cycles completing. Every leaf-level happening is a Change. |

### §5.3 The Pattern

Things dominate at the top, Flows in the middle, Changes at the bottom. The
same three primitives apply at every level — but their **visibility shifts** as
cadence shortens.

### §5.4 Operational Rule

Every artifact gets two tags:

1. **Cadence tag** — one of `50K / 30K / 10K / 5K` (per `ALTITUDE_LEGEND.md`)
2. **Structural altitude tag** — one of `Stratification Layer / Information Flow / State Transition` (per this doc)

When an artifact's structural tag matches the dominant for its cadence, no
comment is required. When it does NOT match (a 50K-cadence Information Flow,
or a 5K-cadence Stratification Layer), the artifact is **unusual for its
altitude** and is worth flagging — it is either a genuine cross-cadence
construct (rare but real) or a misclassification.

### §5.5 What This Does NOT Mean

- **One structural altitude per cadence level** — wrong. All three exist at
  every cadence; only dominance shifts.
- **50K is just a label and the structure lives below** — wrong. 50K is a
  cadence level where Things happen to dominate. Structure applies at every
  cadence.
- **The matrix is a strict assignment table** — wrong. It documents the
  default-and-exception pattern. Most artifacts will sit in the dominant cell
  for their cadence, but cross-cadence constructs are real and supported.
